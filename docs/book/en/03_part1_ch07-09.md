## Chapter 7  Module 5: constrained design screening and continuous optimization

Constrained design screening answers this question: within the limits and search range I give, is there a pond area and outlet size that meets all limits at the same time?

### 7.1 When screening is available

Constrained screening is available only when all of the following hold.

- Only one sharp-crested weir or one rectangular opening is active.
- The coefficients are fixed values.
- The main free-discharge tick, the four checks and the two records are all complete.

If a condition is missing, Module 5 lists what is missing, where to check it and what to enter. These conditions are usually confirmed by the responsible design engineer from drawings or site data.

### 7.2 Limits to enter

![Module 5 constrained-screening settings](manual/en/f08_screening.png)

Figure 7-1 shows the settings of Module 5.

- Allowable peak outflow Qallow (m³/s) and allowable water level Hallow (m): these should come from regulations or design criteria and be written in the "Screening-limit basis".
- Maximum drain-down time Td and permitted residual percentage: at the event end time Te plus Td, the event-added storage must not exceed this percentage of ΔSmax.
- Reference level Href: used only to compute the dimensionless parameters ΠQ and ΠS. It is not a safety limit.
- Search bounds: the minimum and maximum of the weir width b or the opening area Ao. For each ticked size, five equally spaced values are tested within the bounds.

### 7.3 Run and results

Press "Run constrained design screening". The page then switches to the results page. When a feasible case is found, the result lists the selected pond area, the outlet size and a comparison with each limit.

Continuous optimization searches the area A, between its minimum and maximum, together with one outlet size, and then refines locally. Its result is not a proven global optimum.

### 7.4 No feasible case

![Blocked diagnosis when no feasible case exists](manual/en/f10_blocked.png)

Figure 7-2 shows the screen when no feasible case exists. The system shows "Blocked" and the dominant limits (peak outflow, maximum water level or drain-down residual), with "Go to fix" buttons.

Change the pond area or outlet size on an engineering basis, or widen the search bounds where there is a basis for it. Do not relax Qallow or Hallow just to get a solution.

## Chapter 8  Reports and traceable records

- The "Run record for this session (read-only)" on the results page lists the inputs, method, results and limits of this run.
- "Download reproducible decision-support report" saves a text file with the inputs, results and scope of application. You can attach it to the design record.
- When the inputs change, the old results expire. Run again before downloading.
- On the "Start here → Book" tab you can download this book as a PDF or a single web page, and the input data of the Part II examples.

## Chapter 9  Error messages, common questions and the AI assistant

### 9.1 Common questions

| Situation | Cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The run button is grey | A field in Modules 1 to 3 is unreasonable | Read the red list above the button and click a field name to fix it |
| Constrained screening stays locked | More than one outlet, coefficients not fixed, or incomplete free-discharge evidence | Read the list of missing conditions in Module 5 |
| "No feasible case" appears | No design within the bounds meets all limits | Change the area or outlet according to the dominant limit; see Chapter 18 |
| Mass-balance residual above 0.5 % | A numerical problem, often with extreme inputs | Check the input units and the datum; see Chapter 13 |
| Results are marked out of date | Inputs changed after the run | Press "Run routing and evaluation" again |

### 9.2 AI assistant

Press "? Help / AI assistant" at the bottom left to open the AI assistant. It is an offline help assistant. It runs inside your browser, does not connect to any outside AI service and sends no data.

The AI assistant can do three things.

1. Answer operating questions, for example "What should I do next?" or "Why can't I press the button?".
2. Explain terms, for example "What is Qallow?".
3. Search this book: type a keyword (for example "orifice", "RK4" or "no feasible"), and it lists the most relevant sections. Click one to jump to it.

The AI assistant does not change your inputs and makes no engineering judgements. It can tell you how the numbers in the results table compare with the limits, but it will never say "this design can be adopted".
