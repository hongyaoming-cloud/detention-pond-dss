## Chapter 14  Example: how the weir width and pond area affect the results

### 14.1 Problem

In the base case of Chapter 10 the outflow is too large (0.0903 m³/s > 0.080 m³/s). There are two ways to adjust it: change the weir width b, or change the pond area A. This chapter changes one variable at a time and watches how the results change.

### 14.2 Steps

1. Start from the base case (Start here → Book → press "Back to the base case", or reload the page).
2. In Module 3, change the weir width b to 0.10 m, press "Run routing and evaluation" and record the results.
3. Change b to 0.40 m, press "Run routing and evaluation" and record the results.
4. Press "Back to the base case" (b returns to 0.20 m). Then change the area A in Module 2 to 120 m² and press "Run routing and evaluation".
5. Change A to 360 m² and press "Run routing and evaluation" again.

### 14.3 Results

| Change | Qp,o (m³/s) | Hmax (m) | ΔSmax (m³) | RQ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| b = 0.10 m | 0.0732 | 0.570 | 102.635 | 0.610 |
| Base case b = 0.20 m, A = 180 m² | 0.0903 | 0.413 | 74.385 | 0.753 |
| b = 0.40 m | 0.1027 | 0.284 | 51.045 | 0.856 |
| A = 120 m² | 0.1015 | 0.447 | 53.590 | 0.846 |
| A = 360 m² | 0.0636 | 0.327 | 117.754 | 0.530 |

![Hydrographs with a weir width of 0.10 m](book/fig_en/weir_b010_qchart.png)

Figure 14-1 shows the hydrographs for b = 0.10 m. Compared with Figure 10-1, the outflow is flatter and later.

### 14.4 Interpretation

- A narrower weir: the outflow falls, but the water level rises. The water is "held" in the pond, so more detention volume is needed.
- A larger area: both the outflow and the water level fall. The price is more land.
- Both b = 0.10 m and A = 360 m² bring Qp,o below 0.080 m³/s. With the first, the level rises to 0.570 m; with the second, only to 0.327 m. Which is better depends on the land cost and the allowable level.

This is the direction that the "Beginner check" box suggests: a larger pond area usually lowers both the peak water level and the peak outflow; a smaller outlet lowers the peak outflow but raises the peak water level.

2. If Hallow = 0.50 m, which designs in the table above also meet Qallow = 0.080 m³/s?

## Chapter 15  Example: rectangular openings and drain-down time

### 15.1 Problem

Replace the outlet of the base case with a rectangular opening (opening height 0.10 m, invert 0.05 m, Cd = 0.62) and compare three opening areas.

### 15.2 Steps

1. Start from the base case (press "Back to the base case"). In Module 3, untick "Sharp-crested weir" and tick "Rectangular opening".
2. Enter the opening area Ao as 0.005, 0.010 and 0.020 m² in turn, and press "Run routing and evaluation" after each one.

### 15.3 Results

| Ao (m²) | Qp,o (m³/s) | Hmax (m) | ΔSmax (m³) | Storage left at Te + Td |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.005 | 0.0130 | 1.056 | 190.013 | 5.01 % |
| 0.010 | 0.0243 | 0.936 | 168.476 | 5.41 % |
| 0.020 | 0.0428 | 0.752 | 135.278 | 6.67 % |

![Hydrographs with an opening of 0.010 m²](book/fig_en/ori_ao010_qchart.png)

Figure 15-1 shows the hydrographs for Ao = 0.010 m². The outflow is very flat and continues long after the event ends.

### 15.4 Interpretation

- All three openings keep the outflow peak far below 0.080 m³/s, so the peak reduction is strong.
- But the water levels are all high. With Ao = 0.005 m² the level even exceeds the allowable 1.00 m.
- The drain-down matters even more: after 24 hours, more than 5 % of the event-added storage is still in the pond, above the declared 1 %. The main reason is the opening invert of 0.05 m. The water below the opening, 0.05 m × 180 m² = 9 m³, can never flow out, and this is about 5 % of ΔSmax. In addition, the opening releases water very slowly at low levels (Chapter 12). The pond does not drain, so less volume is available when the next storm comes.

This chapter shows that looking only at the outflow peak can lead to a design that does not drain. This is why PondGate treats the drain-down residual as a screening limit. The "Beginner check" also lists the drain-down residual as its third item, and all three openings show "exceeds limit" there.

### 15.5 Exercises

1. When the opening area is doubled, by about what factor does Qp,o change? Why is it not exactly 2?
2. If the drain-down time Td is changed to 48 h, does the residual for Ao = 0.010 m² become larger or smaller? Check it in the software.

## Chapter 16  Example: inflow shape and duration

### 16.1 Problem

Three inflows all have a peak of 0.120 m³/s. The pond and weir are those of the base case; only the inflow shape changes.

| Inflow | Settings | Inflow volume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short triangle (base case) | Tp = Tr = 30 min | 216 m³ |
| Long flat-topped | Rise 24, plateau 72, recession 24 min | 691.2 m³ |
| Long triangle | Tp = Tr = 96 min | 691.2 m³ |

### 16.2 Steps

1. Long flat-topped: in Module 1, set "Standard case" to the long-duration event, keep the other defaults and press "Run routing and evaluation".
2. Long triangle: set the triangular event again, change both Tp and Tr to 96 min and press "Run routing and evaluation".

### 16.3 Results

| Inflow | Qp,o (m³/s) | RQ | Hmax (m) | ΔSmax (m³) | FPI95 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short triangle | 0.0903 | 0.753 | 0.413 | 74.385 | 0.006 |
| Long flat-topped | 0.1200 | 1.000 | 0.499 | 89.890 | 0.036 |
| Long triangle | 0.1123 | 0.936 | 0.478 | 86.018 | 0.011 |

![Hydrographs of the long-duration event](book/fig_en/long_qchart.png)

Figure 16-1 shows the hydrographs of the long flat-topped event. The plateau lasts 72 minutes. The pond fills during the plateau, and the outflow catches up with the inflow.

### 16.4 Interpretation

- The peaks are the same, but the results differ a lot. The long flat-topped event has RQ = 1.000, which means this pond gives no peak reduction at all.
- The reason is the limited capacity of the pond. When the water keeps coming long enough, the pond fills to the equilibrium level where "outflow equals inflow", and the detention effect disappears.
- The long-duration events have a larger FPI95, which means the top of the outflow is flat. The "time of peak" then has little meaning; use the median-volume time and the discharge-centroid time instead.

So a design cannot rely on the peak discharge alone. For the same peak, check the most unfavourable duration and shape.

1. In the long flat-topped event, what is the water level when the outflow equals the inflow? Work it back from the weir equation in Chapter 12.
2. If the plateau time is changed to 36 minutes, how does RQ change?

## Chapter 17  Example: an irregular pond and a stage–storage table

### 17.1 Problem

Real ponds often have sloped sides or irregular shapes, so the area changes with the level. In that case, describe the pond with a stage–storage table. This example uses the data of Table 17-1; all other conditions are the base case.

| Water level H (m) | 0 | 0.25 | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 | 1.50 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Storage S (m³) | 0 | 30 | 80 | 150 | 240 | 420 |

This table is also in the download package as ch17_irregular_stage_storage.csv.

### 17.2 Steps

1. Start from the base case (press "Back to the base case"). In Module 2, set the storage representation to the tabulated stage–storage relation.
2. Paste Table 17-1 into the table field (one "level, storage" pair per line), or import the CSV file.
3. Press "Run routing and evaluation".

### 17.3 Results

| Pond shape | Qp,o (m³/s) | Hmax (m) | ΔSmax (m³) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Constant area 180 m² (base case) | 0.0903 | 0.413 | 74.385 |
| Irregular pond (Table 17-1) | 0.0912 | 0.416 | 63.161 |

### 17.4 Interpretation

The irregular pond is smaller at low levels (the mean area between 0 and 0.25 m is only 120 m²). The water level rises faster, so the outlet starts to release a lot of water earlier. At 0.416 m the irregular pond holds only about 63 m³, while the constant-area pond holds about 75 m³. Less water stays in the pond and more is released, so Qp,o is slightly higher and the detention volume is about 11 m³ smaller, while the peak levels are almost the same.

When you use a stage–storage table, note the following:

- The first row is usually the pond floor (S = 0).
- Routing does not extrapolate beyond the table. The highest level in the table must be above the highest possible water level; otherwise the result is invalid.
- The table and the outlet elevations must use the same datum.

### 17.5 Exercises

1. From Table 17-1, compute the mean area between 0.25 and 0.50 m.
2. What happens if the table only reaches H = 0.40 m?
