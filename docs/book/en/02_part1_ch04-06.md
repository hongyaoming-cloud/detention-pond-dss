## Chapter 4  Five-minute start

This chapter uses the "triangular event" demonstration case, which is loaded when the software opens, to complete your first routing run. The demonstration case is for practice only and does not stand for any real project.

1. Open PondGate and switch to English if needed. Check that the tab bar is on "Setup and routing".
2. Scroll down to Module 4 at the bottom of the page and press "Run routing and evaluation".
3. The page switches to "Results, verification and records".
4. In the "Beginner check" box above the results, read what the system says about Hmax and Qp,o.
5. In the results table, first check that the "Mass-balance residual ε/Vin" is close to 0 %. Then read the peak inflow, the peak outflow and the peak water level.
6. Press "Download reproducible decision-support report" to save the record of this run to your computer.

![Inflow and routed outflow hydrographs](manual/guide_en/g03_06_qchart.png)

Figure 4-1 shows the inflow and outflow hydrographs of the demonstration case. The outflow peak is lower than the inflow peak and comes later. This is the "peak reduction" and "delay" effect of a detention pond.

Table 4-1 lists the numbers you should see. If the numbers are the same, you did it right.

| Item | Value you should see |
| --- | --- |
| Peak inflow Qp,i | 0.1200 m³/s |
| Peak outflow Qp,o | 0.0903 m³/s |
| Peak water level Hmax | 0.413 m |
| Mass-balance residual ε/Vin | 0.000 % |
| Beginner check | Not good enough |

The default allowable outflow Qallow of the demonstration case is 0.080 m³/s. The peak outflow of 0.0903 m³/s is above this value, so the "Beginner check" says "Not good enough". This is the expected result, not an operating error. Chapters 10 and 14 explain the reason and how to improve the design.

There are two ways to reset all inputs to the demonstration case. First, open "Start here → Book" and press "Back to the base case". Second, reload the web page. Both clear any unsaved changes. After a reload, switch to English again if the page opens in Chinese.

Readers who want to follow every step with a screenshot can open "Start here → Full step-by-step guide", Chapter 3.

## Chapter 5  Modules 1 to 3: describe the inflow, the pond and the outlet

### 5.1 Module 1: inflow boundary

![Module 1 inflow boundary](manual/en/f03_inflow.png)

Figure 5-1 shows Module 1. The inflow can be described in three ways.

- Triangular event: enter the peak inflow Qp, the time to peak Tp and the recession time Tr.
- Long-duration event: enter the rise, plateau and recession times to form a trapezoidal hydrograph.
- Imported time–discharge pairs: one "time (min), discharge (m³/s)" pair per line. The first time must be 0, times must increase, discharges must not be negative, and at least two lines are needed.

The total event duration T is the sum of the parts and cannot be entered directly. Remember that the peak discharge alone is not enough: the duration and shape of the hydrograph also change the detention volume needed (Chapter 16 shows an example).

### 5.2 Module 2: stage–storage relation

![Module 2 storage–water-level state](manual/en/f04_storage.png)

Figure 5-2 shows Module 2. Storage can be described in two ways.

- Constant plan area: S(H) = A × (H − Hfloor). Only the area A is needed.
- Tabulated stage–storage relation: import a two-column .csv or .txt file, with the water level (m) in the first column and the storage (m³) in the second. Routing does not extrapolate beyond the table.

The floor elevation Hfloor, the initial level H0 and all outlet elevations must use the same datum. With a relative datum, set Hfloor = 0.

### 5.3 Module 3: outlet hydraulics and free-discharge evidence

![Module 3 outlet hydraulics and free-discharge evidence](manual/en/f05_outlet.png)

Figure 5-3 shows Module 3. Only two outlet types can enter formal constrained screening.

- Sharp-crested weir: Qout = Cw × b × (H − P)^1.5. Enter the weir coefficient Cw, the weir width b and the crest elevation P.
- Rectangular opening: enter the discharge coefficient Cd, the opening area Ao, the opening height and the invert elevation. Before the water rises above the top of the opening, the opening acts like a narrow weir. After that, the discharge increases with head according to the orifice equation. Chapter 12 gives the full equations.

Risers, circular pipes, stage-dependent coefficient tables and combined outlets (weir and opening together) are for "research inspection". You can route them and read the results, but constrained design screening is locked.

You can use the default coefficients (Cw = 1.70, Cd = 0.62) or enter calibrated site values. The defaults are suitable only for preliminary screening.

The free-discharge evidence consists of one main tick, four checks (tailwater, backwater and pipe friction, submergence, operational control) and two text records. Tick them only when you really have drawings, site checks or calculations to support them. The software does not verify these conditions for you.

## Chapter 6  Modules 4 and 6: run the routing and read the results

### 6.1 Run

Press "Run routing and evaluation" in Module 4 to start the calculation. If the button is grey, a field in Modules 1 to 3 has an unreasonable value. The fields to fix are listed above the button. Click one to jump to it; it is outlined in red.

Common causes are: Qp less than or equal to 0, an area of 0, a weir width or coefficient of 0, an outlet elevation below the floor, or no active outlet.

### 6.2 Beginner check

The "Beginner check" box at the top of the results page compares three results directly with the limits you declared: the peak water level Hmax with the allowable level Hallow, the peak outflow Qp,o with the allowable outflow Qallow, and the residual after the drain-down time Td with the allowable residual percentage.

- Green: all three are below their limits.
- Yellow: a value equals its limit and there is no safety margin. Treat it with caution.
- Red: a limit is exceeded. The box suggests a direction: a larger pond area usually lowers both the peak water level and the peak outflow; a smaller outlet lowers the peak outflow but raises the peak water level.

The beginner check only compares readings. It is not an engineering approval.

### 6.3 Order for reading the results table

![Results table](manual/guide_en/g03_07_table.png)

Figure 6-1 shows the results table. Read it in this order.

1. Mass-balance residual ε/Vin: it should be close to 0 %. Above 0.5 %, the system asks you to solve the numerical problem first, and the results must not be used.
2. Peak outflow Qp,o and peak ratio RQ = Qp,o ÷ Qp,i: a smaller RQ means more peak reduction.
3. Peak water level Hmax and event-added detention volume ΔSmax: compare them with the allowable level and the freeboard.
4. Near-peak flatness FPI95: a large value means a very flat peak. A single peak time is then not reliable; use the volume-based timing measures instead.

### 6.4 When the inputs change, the results expire

If an input changes after a run, the screen marks the results as out of date. Press "Run routing and evaluation" again before you interpret them. Ticking the free-discharge evidence or filling in the record fields does not make the routing results expire, because these fields do not change the computed numbers.
