## Chapter 10  The role of a detention pond and the design limits

### 10.1 Principle

After development, the ground surface is less permeable, so the same storm produces a larger and faster flood peak. A detention pond stores part of the water and releases it slowly, so the peak seen downstream becomes smaller and later.

A design usually has two limits.

- Allowable outflow Qallow: the largest outflow allowed by the downstream drainage system or by regulations.
- Allowable water level Hallow: the upper limit of the water level in the pond, usually set by the pond depth, the freeboard or the surrounding ground levels.

A design is "good enough" only when Qp,o ≤ Qallow and Hmax ≤ Hallow at the same time, and the event-added storage left in the pond after the required drain-down time Td does not exceed the declared percentage.

### 10.2 Example: is the base case good enough?

This example assumes Qallow = 0.080 m³/s and Hallow = 1.00 m (teaching assumption).

1. Open PondGate and keep the base case (if you have changed any input, press "Back to the base case" first).
2. In Module 5, enter Qallow = 0.080 and Hallow = 1.00.
3. Go back to Module 4 and press "Run routing and evaluation".

![Inflow and outflow hydrographs of the base case](book/fig_en/demo_qchart.png)

Figure 10-1 shows the inflow and outflow hydrographs of the base case. The results page reads as follows.

| Item | Result | Limit | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Peak outflow Qp,o | 0.0903 m³/s | Qallow = 0.0800 m³/s | Exceeds |

The "Beginner check" box says "Not good enough", because the peak outflow exceeds the allowable value. The water level still has a large margin. This means the pond is not too small; the outlet is too large and releases water too fast. Chapter 14 uses the same case to find a direction for improvement.

### 10.3 Key points

- To judge "good enough", read the outflow, the water level and the drain-down together. Reading only one of them can mislead you.
- When one limit is exceeded and another has a large margin, the pond area and the outlet are usually not well balanced.

## Chapter 11  Hydraulic basics: the continuity equation and storage

### 11.1 Principle

The change of the water volume in the pond equals the inflow minus the outflow:

```formula
dS/dt = Qin(t) − Qout(H)
```

With a constant plan area, the storage is proportional to the water level:

```formula
S(H) = A × (H − Hfloor)
```

Together these two equations are called "level-pool routing". It assumes that the water surface is level at every moment. It suits detention ponds with a large area, shallow water and low velocity.

### 11.2 Hand calculation: inflow volume

The volume of a triangular hydrograph is the area of the triangle:

```formula
Vin = ½ × Qp × (Tp + Tr) = ½ × 0.120 × 3600 = 216 m³
```

The software computes an event-added detention volume ΔSmax = 74.385 m³, so the storage ratio is RS = 74.385 ÷ 216 = 0.344, the same as in the results table. In other words, at most about one third of the inflow volume was in the pond at the same time.

### 11.3 An important property: the outflow peak lies on the falling limb of the inflow

When the outflow depends only on the water level in the pond (free discharge), dS/dt = 0 at the moment of the highest level, so Qin = Qout. Therefore the outflow peak always lies on the falling limb of the inflow hydrograph.

Check with the base case: an independent program puts the outflow peak at t = 37.4 min, with Qout = 0.0903 m³/s. The inflow at that moment is:

```formula
Qin = 0.120 × (60 − 37.4) ÷ 30 = 0.0904 m³/s
```

The two differ by less than 0.0001 m³/s; the difference comes from rounding the time to 0.1 min. In Figure 10-1 the highest point of the outflow curve touches the falling line of the inflow. This is the property at work.

### 11.4 Key points

- The moment of the highest water level is the moment of the largest outflow, and also the moment when Qin = Qout.
- If another program shows an outflow peak that is not on the falling limb of the inflow, first check whether the outlet is affected by downstream conditions.

## Chapter 12  Outlet equations: weirs and rectangular openings

### 12.1 Sharp-crested weir

```formula
Qout = Cw × b × (H − P)^1.5
```

In the base case, Cw × b = 1.70 × 0.20 = 0.34. Table 12-1 lists the discharge at different heads.

| Head H − P (m) | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.40 | 0.50 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weir flow Qout (m³/s) | 0.0108 | 0.0304 | 0.0559 | 0.0860 | 0.1202 |

The discharge grows with the 1.5 power of the head, so a small rise in level greatly increases the outflow.

### 12.2 Rectangular opening

PondGate computes a rectangular opening in two ranges. The opening height is a, the width is w = Ao ÷ a, and the head is y = H − invert elevation.

- The water has not risen above the top of the opening (y ≤ a): the opening acts as a weir of width w, Q = Cw × w × y^1.5.
- The water is above the top of the opening (y > a): the discharge starts from the weir flow at the top of the opening and adds the increase given by the orifice equation. The discharge is therefore continuous at the transition point and does not jump:

```formula
Q = Cw × w × a^1.5 + Cd × w × a × [ √(2g(y − a/2)) − √(2g × a/2) ]
```

Take Ao = 0.010 m², a = 0.10 m, invert 0.05 m, Cw = 1.70 and Cd = 0.62 as an example. Table 12-2 compares the discharges of the weir and the opening.

| Water level H (m) | 0.15 | 0.30 | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weir b = 0.20 m (m³/s) | 0.0198 | 0.0559 | 0.1202 | 0.2208 | 0.3400 |
| Opening Ao = 0.010 m² (m³/s) | 0.0054 | 0.0115 | 0.0166 | 0.0214 | 0.0253 |

The discharge of the opening grows only with about the 0.5 power of the head. As the level rises, a weir releases water quickly, while an opening releases it slowly. This difference drives the design behaviour in Chapters 14 and 15.

### 12.3 Coefficients and free discharge

Cw = 1.70 and Cd = 0.62 are common preliminary values. The coefficients of a real structure depend on its shape, edges and approach velocity. A formal design should use calibrated values.

All these equations assume free discharge: the downstream water level is low enough not to back up into the outlet. If the outlet may be submerged from downstream, the equations no longer hold. This is why constrained screening requires the free-discharge evidence.

### 12.4 Exercises

2. When the water level at the opening rises from 0.50 m to 1.00 m, by what percentage does the discharge increase? And for the weir?

## Chapter 13  Numerical method: RK4, time step and mass balance

### 13.1 Principle

The continuity equation has no general analytical solution, so it is integrated numerically step by step. PondGate uses the fourth-order Runge–Kutta method (RK4), which computes four slopes in each step:

```formula
k1 = f(t, S)
k2 = f(t + Δt/2, S + Δt × k1/2)
k3 = f(t + Δt/2, S + Δt × k2/2)
k4 = f(t + Δt, S + Δt × k3)
S(next) = S + Δt/6 × (k1 + 2k2 + 2k3 + k4)
```

Here f(t, S) = Qin(t) − Qout(H(S)), and the PondGate step is Δt = 2 s.

### 13.2 One step by hand

Take the base case at t = 1800 s (the time of the inflow peak). At this moment S = 65.608 m³, H = 0.3645 m, Qin = 0.1200 m³/s and Qout = 0.0748 m³/s.

| Slope | Value (m³/s) |
| --- | --- |
| k1 | 0.045181 |
| k2 | 0.045037 |
| k3 | 0.045038 |
| k4 | 0.044894 |

Substituting gives S_next = 65.608 + 2 ÷ 6 × (0.045181 + 2 × 0.045037 + 2 × 0.045038 + 0.044894) = 65.699 m³, and the water level rises to 0.3650 m. The four slopes are very close, which shows that a 2-second step is very small for this case.

### 13.3 Time-step sensitivity

Table 13-1 compares results for different steps from an independent program. RK4 hardly changes for steps up to 60 s. The simple forward Euler method loses accuracy quickly as the step grows.

| Method and step | Qp,o (m³/s) | Hmax (m) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RK4, 2 s (PondGate) | 0.09032 | 0.4132 |
| RK4, 60 s | 0.09028 | 0.4131 |
| RK4, 300 s | 0.08896 | 0.4091 |
| Euler, 60 s | 0.09179 | 0.4177 |
| Euler, 300 s | 0.09910 | 0.4396 |

The peaks for large steps in the table are also affected by sampling: with a 300-second step, the result is seen only every 5 minutes.

### 13.4 Mass-balance residual

The mass-balance residual ε/Vin checks whether "cumulative inflow − cumulative outflow" equals the change in storage. For the base case the absolute residual is 1.259 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³, and the relative residual is shown as 0.000 %. Above 0.5 %, the results must not be used; check the inputs first.

### 13.5 How the numbers in this book are checked

Every example in Part II was run in the software by docs/book/run_examples.js, which read the results from the screen. docs/book/check_examples.py then recomputed them with an independently written RK4 program. The two agree on Qp,o, Hmax, ΔSmax and the drain-down residual in every case (Qp,o differs by less than 0.00005 m³/s and Hmax by less than 0.0005 m). When one number is changed on purpose, the check program reports an error.
